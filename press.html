In one paragraph

LeLau is an AI assistant that runs entirely on your iPhone. It answers with no connection, from knowledge packs you build out of your own PDFs and notes, or install from someone else. There is no account, no server and no telemetry: LeLau is built so that nobody, including its developer, can see what you ask. Siri answers by voice without opening the app. Optional web lookup goes through an embedded Tor client and fails closed. The app is free, with no ads and no in-app purchases.

In one sentence

AI that works on a plane, and no one, not even us, can see what you asked.

Facts

  • Platform: iPhone 14 Pro or newer (6 GB RAM), iOS 16 and later. Download is about 840 MB; use Wi-Fi.
  • Price: free. No ads, no subscription, no in-app purchases.
  • Models on the phone: Qwen3.5 0.8B for answers; a 0.6B embedding model and an int8 reranker for retrieval. An optional High Accuracy engine (Gemma 4 E2B, 2.8 GB) installs only after you approve the download.
  • Privacy: no account, no analytics, no LeLau server. The App Store privacy label reads "Data Not Collected". Settings includes an airplane-mode test so you can check the claim yourself.
  • Network: normal chat uses none. Web lookup and model downloads, both optional, go through an embedded Tor client and fail closed rather than falling back to a direct connection.
  • Siri: "Hey Siri, ask LeLau a question" answers from your packs with the app closed. "Have LeLau learn a topic" builds a pack in the background.
  • Packs: local knowledge libraries. Five ship with the app: Survival Guide, First Aid & Medicine, Food Water & Shelter, Rebuilding Technology, Disasters & Climates. Packs share phone to phone by AirDrop with no server involved.
  • Released: version 1.0, September 2026, on the App Store.

Founder

LeLau is built by Leonardo Calancea, an independent developer in The Hague, Netherlands. It started from two questions: what happens to the questions you would never type into a cloud AI, and why does every assistant stop working the moment you lose signal.

What we would rather you did not say

LeLau is not anonymous and does not make you untraceable; Tor hides the phone's address from the sites it fetches, nothing more. It is not smarter than cloud models. Its answers can be wrong, and the app says so.
